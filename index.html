01 Approach

What companies actually get

Not architecture diagrams. Working systems, owned by the team after I leave, tied to a number the business already cares about.

  1. 01

    Cloud spend you can explain, line by line

    Billing data from AWS, Azure and GCP normalised into one model, allocated to the teams and products that caused it. The result is a number every engineering manager recognises as theirs — which is the only version of showback anyone acts on. Typical outcome: 20–30% of spend identified as recoverable in the first pass.

  2. 02

    Legacy systems moved without the outage

    Oracle, Teradata and mainframe estates migrated to Snowflake and Databricks with CDC-based cutover, reconciliation testing and a rollback path. I've taken a 300+ table Oracle estate into Snowflake for a regulated financial client with no business downtime, and refactored 50K+ lines of COBOL into PySpark.

  3. 03

    Pipelines that hold under SLA

    Streaming and batch ingestion on Kafka, Delta Live Tables, Airflow and Argo Workflows, moving 50M+ records a day with ACID guarantees — built with the alerting, lineage and quality gates that decide whether anyone trusts the output at 3am on a Sunday.

  4. 04

    AI in the pipeline, where it earns its place

    LangGraph agents for data quality triage, governed natural-language SQL, and cost models that explain their own reasoning. I add a model when it beats a SQL query, and I build the confidence and refusal layer before the demo — because an LLM that answers confidently and wrongly is worse than no answer.

  5. 05

    A team that doesn't need me afterwards

    dbt standards, documentation, CI and review practice that outlive the engagement. At a regulated financial client the dbt workflow I introduced was adopted as the standard across squads. Handover is part of the work, not a phase at the end that gets cut.

Multicloud FinOps Intelligence

Framework

Problem
Three clouds, three billing formats, no shared definition of cost. Teams could see the invoice but never the driver behind it.

LangGraph Data Quality Copilot

Repository ↗

Agent system

Problem
Quality failures surfaced far downstream, and root-cause investigation consumed the most senior engineer on the team every time.
Approach
A LangGraph agent running on local LLMs that profiles DuckDB datasets, writes its own validation rules, runs Great Expectations and explains what broke.

~70% less triage time, and failure reports a data steward can act on without reading a stack trace.

03

Intent-Aware Cloud Governance

Repository ↗

Research framework

Problem
Cost governance is retrospective by design. By the time an overspend is visible on an invoice, the money is already gone.
Approach
Read the intent behind a provisioning request, simulate its cost, and apply policy at the moment of the decision rather than after billing.
Result
Prevention instead of reporting — an open framework for pricing a change before it becomes a line item.
PythonPolicy modelling Cost simulationLLM reasoning

Trust-Aware Metadata Intelligence

Repository ↗

Text-to-SQL

Problem
Analysts lose hours writing SQL against undocumented schemas — and text-to-SQL tools answer with total confidence even when they are wrong.
Approach
Generation grounded in vector-embedded schema metadata and curated examples, wrapped in ambiguity detection and confidence propagation.
Result
A system that refuses rather than guesses — schema-aware SQL with a governance layer that knows the limits of its own answer.

PersistBench

Repository ↗

Benchmark

Problem
Agent safety benchmarks test one session at a time. Real attacks on tool-using agents persist across sessions and hide inside memory.
Approach
A scenario suite for persistent, cross-session adversarial attacks on tool-augmented LLM agents, with layered defences and a governance evaluation pipeline.
Result
The first benchmark of its kind — 77 scenarios across 7 defences, fully reproducible.

06 About

The route here was unusual

Background

I started on COBOL, JCL and DB2 mainframes in shipping. Fourteen years later I design the FinOps systems that tell a company where its cloud money goes. The distance between those two jobs is most of what I know.

That route turns out to be useful. Enterprises rarely have a clean data problem — they have a forty-year-old system nobody wants to touch, sitting under the number the board looks at every quarter. Having written the COBOL and having built the lakehouse, I can read both ends of that and tell you honestly which parts are worth moving.

Today I'm Staff Data Engineer at ServiceNow, leading AI-driven FinOps analytics across AWS, Azure and GCP. Before that: a zero-downtime Oracle-to-Snowflake migration for a regulated financial services firm, HIPAA-compliant patient analytics for a national healthcare provider, and real-time freight pipelines for a Class I railroad.

Outside that, I publish research on cloud cost governance and the safety of tool-using LLM agents. I care about systems that are honest about their own uncertainty — a cost model that shows its assumptions, an agent that refuses rather than guesses. Confidence without calibration is the expensive failure mode, in FinOps and in AI both.

Based in Houston, Texas. Open to senior and staff data engineering roles, and to advisory work on FinOps and platform migration.
